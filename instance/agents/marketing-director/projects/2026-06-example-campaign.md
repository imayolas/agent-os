# Example Campaign — How the Squad Works Together

> Demonstration project. It shows the **delegate → integrate** loop that makes a squad more than a single agent. Delete it when you start real work (`status: done`, then remove the file — git keeps the history).

## The CEO's ask

> "I want to launch a content + paid push around [topic] next month."

The CEO (the user) hands this to me, the Marketing Director. I don't write copy, research keywords, or build ad campaigns myself — I **break it into briefs and delegate**, then assemble the pieces.

## How a lead runs this (the teamwork loop)

1. **Scope it (me).** Turn the CEO's one-liner into an objective, audience, and success metric. Decide the sequence.
2. **Delegate research → SEO Analyst.** "Find the 5 best keyword angles for [topic], with intent and difficulty. Return a one-page brief." The SEO Analyst runs in its own context, does the digging, writes findings to its own artifacts, and returns a summary.
3. **Chain the brief → Content Writer.** Hand the SEO Analyst's brief to the Content Writer: "Draft a pillar post against this brief, brand voice, ~1500 words." The Writer drafts and returns it.
4. **Delegate the paid plan → PPC Expert.** "Propose a paid campaign to amplify this post: channel, budget split, 3 ad variants." Returns a plan.
5. **Integrate (me).** I assemble research + draft + paid plan into one coherent campaign and bring **decisions** back to the CEO — not raw work. The CEO approves what ships.

```
CEO (you)
  │  "launch a push around [topic]"
  ▼
Marketing Director ── delegates ──▶ SEO Analyst   (keyword brief)
        │                          ▶ Content Writer (draft, fed the brief)
        │                          ▶ PPC Expert     (paid plan)
        ◀──────── returns ─────────  concise summaries
  │  integrates → recommends
  ▼
CEO approves what ships
```

## Why this beats one do-everything agent

Each specialist reasons in a **fresh, focused context** with its own expertise and memory — no context pollution, deeper work, and the lead stays strategic instead of drowning in detail. That separation of concerns is the whole point of squad-based work.
